## THE NEUROSCIENCE OF ATTENTION

Human attention follows predictable patterns:
- **0–3 seconds**: Pattern recognition. The brain decides "is this for me?" based on visual + audio signals. No hook = scroll.
- **3–15 seconds**: Investment phase. Viewer decides whether to commit. Must deliver on the hook promise.
- **15–30 seconds**: Retention test. First drop-off window. Re-hook needed or introduce conflict/tension.
- **30–60 seconds**: Deep engagement. Viewers who reach here are invested. Reward with value.
- **60s+**: Loyalty window. Only the most engaged viewers remain. Can introduce complexity.

**Dopamine triggers that increase watch time:**
- Unexplained visual → explanation payoff
- Question raised → answer withheld
- Conflict introduced → resolution teased
- Pattern established → pattern broken
- Progress shown → completion implied

## HOOK FRAMEWORKS

### The 7 Hook Archetypes

1. **Curiosity Gap**: "You won't believe what happened when I..." / Show something surprising before explaining it
2. **Bold Claim**: "I tried X for 30 days and..." / State a counterintuitive result upfront
3. **Relatability Punch**: Open with a universal frustration/experience before offering your take
4. **Visual Disruption**: Something visually unexpected in the first frame (color, movement, scale)
5. **Direct Address**: Break the fourth wall — talk directly to the viewer's specific situation
6. **Story In Media Res**: Start in the middle of action, then rewind to explain
7. **Controversy/Contrast**: "Everyone says X but..." / Position against common belief

### Hook Scoring Criteria
- **Excellent (90-100)**: Combines visual disruption + curiosity gap + direct relevance
- **Strong (70-89)**: Two of the above elements
- **Moderate (50-69)**: One element present, executed well
- **Weak (20-49)**: Talking head with no hook element
- **None (0-19)**: Slow buildup, intro screens, logo reveals

## PACING & RHYTHM

### Cuts Per Minute (CPM) by Platform

| Platform | Ideal CPM | Energy Level |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| TikTok | 20–35 | High-very high |
| Instagram Reels | 15–25 | Medium-high |
| YouTube Shorts | 12–20 | Medium |
| YouTube Long-form | 4–10 | Varies by section |
| LinkedIn | 6–12 | Professional, measured |

### Pacing Principles
- **Breath beats**: Every 30–45 seconds, insert a 1–2 second "breath" — slight pause, slower visual, calmer audio — before ramping back up. Prevents cognitive overload.
- **Energy escalation**: Videos should generally escalate in energy toward the conclusion, not front-load all energy.
- **Pattern interrupts**: Every 8–12 seconds, introduce a change: cut, zoom, sound effect, text pop, camera angle change, visual effect. Prevents habituation.
- **Cut on motion**: Cuts feel more natural when timed to movement (head turn, hand gesture, audio beat).
- **J-cut / L-cut**: Audio transitions before/after visual cuts create smoother, more cinematic flow.

### Common Pacing Mistakes
- Long static shots (>3 seconds) in fast-paced content
- Consistent clip length without variation (feels robotic)
- Energy drop after the hook (the "valley of death" at 10–20 seconds)
- Ending energy lower than middle energy

## RETENTION ENGINEERING

### The Retention Curve Architecture

**0–3s**: Visual + audio hook fires. Target: 95%+ retention.
**3–10s**: Promise delivery or intensification. Target: 85%+.
**10–30s**: First narrative beat resolves, second begins. Target: 70%+.
**30–60s**: Sustained value delivery with re-hooks. Target: 50%+.
**60s+**: Core message + CTA. Target: 30%+ (excellent), 20%+ (good).

### Re-hook Techniques (use every 15–20 seconds)
- "But here's the thing..." (introduces complication)
- "Wait, it gets better..." (promises payoff)
- Text overlay: "Part 2 is even crazier"
- Visual zoom or sudden cut to different angle
- Sound effect + visual pop
- Direct address: "If you're still watching, you need to hear this"

### Drop-off Red Flags
- Long sentences without visual variation
- Information overload without pacing
- Repetitive framing (same camera angle for >10 seconds)
- Background music that doesn't match energy
- Complex explanation without visual support (B-roll, text, graphics)
- Outro-like energy before content is done

## PLATFORM-SPECIFIC INTELLIGENCE

### TikTok

**Algorithm signals**: Completion rate > shares > comments > likes
**Optimal length**: 15–34 seconds (maximum algorithm boost), up to 60s for strong content
**Native features that boost reach**: TikTok sounds (trending), text overlays using TikTok font, duet/stitch hooks, "For You" framing
**Visual style**: Vertical 9:16, high saturation, fast cuts, Gen Z aesthetic (authentic > polished)
**Audio**: Native TikTok sounds or trending music increases distribution 40%. NEVER use copyrighted music from other platforms.
**Hook formula that works**: Extreme close-up face + controversial statement or visual surprise
**Caption style**: Large, high-contrast text, middle of screen, 1–4 words per pop
**What kills TikTok performance**: Horizontal video, watermarks from other platforms, low-res footage, slow intros, "follow me" CTAs too early

### Instagram Reels

**Algorithm signals**: Shares to Stories > saves > comments > likes
**Optimal length**: 15–30 seconds (max reach), 30–90s (storytelling)
**Aesthetic**: Higher production value acceptable, aesthetic consistency matters more than TikTok
**Audio**: Original audio or licensed Instagram music. Trending audio boosts discovery.
**Text**: Aesthetic, on-brand typography. Lower third or centered.
**What works**: Educational/informational content, before/after, transformation, aesthetic B-roll + VO
**What kills Reels performance**: TikTok watermarks, poor audio quality, no subtitles (70% watch without sound)

### YouTube Shorts

**Algorithm signals**: Like rate + subscription rate + watch time percentage
**Optimal length**: 45–59 seconds (keeps high percentage completion while providing depth)
**Unique opportunity**: Can funnel viewers to long-form content on same channel
**Hook**: More explanation acceptable than TikTok — viewers are more patient
**Audio**: Can use YouTube's music library. Voice clarity is very important.
**Text**: YouTube auto-captions are important — ensure clean audio for accurate captions
**What works**: Tutorial snippets, highlights from long videos, standalone tips, controversy clips
**Vertical CTA**: "Watch the full video" or "Subscribe for more" performs well here

## SOUND DESIGN

### The Audio Stack (layering audio for professional feel)

1. **Primary audio** (voice/main sound): 0 dB reference, clear, no clipping
2. **Music bed**: -18 to -24 dB, energy-matched to visual rhythm
3. **Sound effects / foley**: -12 to -6 dB, timed to visual impacts
4. **Whooshes / transitions**: -10 to -8 dB, on every major cut
5. **Text pop SFX**: -18 to -14 dB, one per text element

### Music Selection Rules
- Energy of music = energy of visuals. Mismatch = friction.
- BPM of music should align with CPM (cuts per minute)
- Music drops / buildups should align with major content transitions
- Silence (no music) for 1–3 seconds before a big reveal increases impact

### SFX Timing
- Whoosh: 2–4 frames BEFORE the cut (anticipation)
- Impact: On the first frame of the new clip
- Text pop: Exactly on text appearance (1 frame tolerance)
- Transition: Centered on the cut point

### Voice/Audio Quality
- Noise floor should be below -50 dB
- Voice should sit at -12 to -6 dB peak
- EQ: high-pass filter at 80–100 Hz (remove rumble), slight boost at 2–4 kHz (presence)
- Compression: ratio 3:1 to 4:1 for broadcast voice feel

## STORYTELLING FRAMEWORKS

### Compressed Hero's Journey (for short-form)
1. **Normal world** (0–2s): Establish context/character
2. **Problem** (2–5s): The conflict/challenge/tension
3. **Transformation** (5–15s): The journey / discovery
4. **New world** (15–20s): The result / revelation / punchline

### PAS (Problem-Agitation-Solution)
- Problem: Name a specific, relatable pain
- Agitation: Twist the knife — make it feel more urgent/relevant
- Solution: Your method/approach/product/insight

### BAB (Before-After-Bridge)
- Before: The struggle/situation most people are in
- After: The transformation/result (show, don't tell)
- Bridge: Your specific mechanism that creates the change

### The AIDA Framework (for informational content)
- Attention: Visual/audio hook
- Interest: Relevant context + why this matters to viewer
- Desire: Show the transformation/outcome
- Action: Clear CTA (save, follow, click, try)

## B-ROLL STRATEGY

### B-roll Placement Rules
- Cover every cut between talking head shots
- Insert B-roll at points of potential drop-off (10s, 20s, 30s)
- B-roll should visually ILLUSTRATE what's being said (not just generic footage)
- Maximum 3 seconds of B-roll before returning to primary visual
- Zoom into B-roll details — static B-roll is worse than no B-roll

### B-roll Types and When to Use
- **Demonstration footage**: Best for tutorials/how-to content
- **Reaction shots**: Creates emotional connection in storytelling
- **Environment/context shots**: Establishes credibility and place
- **Text/graphics**: When concept is abstract or complex
- **Stock footage**: Last resort — must match color grade and aesthetic

## COLOR GRADING PRINCIPLES

### Platform-Specific Color Aesthetics

| Platform | Aesthetic | LUT Style |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| TikTok | Vibrant, high contrast, slight oversaturation | Film-look or clean bright |
| Instagram | Warm, cohesive, consistent with feed | Warm golden or moody muted |
| YouTube | Clean, natural, professional | Slightly warm, natural skin tones |

### Lumetri Quick-Win Settings
- **Exposure**: Start at 0, adjust by ±0.3 for mood
- **Contrast**: +20 to +40 for most platforms (adds punch)
- **Highlights**: -20 to -40 (recover blown highlights)
- **Shadows**: +10 to +20 (lift shadows, improves mobile viewing)
- **Whites**: +5 to +15 (adds brightness)
- **Blacks**: -10 to -20 (adds depth)
- **Saturation**: +10 to +20 for social (boost vibrancy)
- **Temperature**: +5 to +15 (warm = more engaging for most content)

## VIRALITY SIGNALS

### What Makes Content Shareable
1. **Identity expression**: "This is so me" / "This is exactly how I feel"
2. **Useful information**: "I need to save this" / "My friends need to see this"
3. **Emotional resonance**: Makes viewer feel something strongly (funny, inspired, shocked, validated)
4. **Controversy**: Disagrees with a common belief in a way that sparks debate
5. **Novelty**: Shows something the viewer has never seen before
6. **Aspiration**: Shows a transformation/result the viewer wants

### The Viral Structure
The highest-performing short-form videos follow this pattern:
1. **Attention spike** (0–2s): Pattern interrupt or curiosity gap
2. **Identification moment** (2–5s): Viewer recognizes themselves in the content
3. **Value escalation** (5–15s): Stakes or information increases
4. **Punchline/Payoff** (15–25s): The "aha" moment or emotional peak
5. **CTA** (last 2–3s): Frictionless — save, follow, or share

### Content That Consistently Underperforms
- Generic motivational content without specific insight
- Overproduced content that feels inauthentic
- Content that tries to appeal to everyone
- Slow builds without an early hook
- CTAs that ask for too much (subscribe AND like AND comment AND share)
- Following trends without a unique angle

## AUDIENCE PSYCHOLOGY

### The 3 Core Viewer Needs
1. **Entertainment**: Make me feel something (laugh, gasp, cry, inspired)
2. **Education**: Teach me something I didn't know that helps my life
3. **Belonging**: Make me feel understood, seen, part of a community

Every high-performing video satisfies at least 2 of these 3 needs.

### Viewer Decision Points (moments when they consider leaving)
- First 0.5 seconds: Visual quality + audio quality assessment
- First 3 seconds: Topic relevance assessment
- Every 8–12 seconds: "Is this still worth my time?" re-evaluation
- After any confusion: Cognitive load exceeded → leave
- After any lull: Energy drop → scroll

### Trust Signals
- Consistent aesthetic = expertise and professionalism
- Specific details > vague claims (numbers, names, dates)
- Vulnerability and authenticity > polished perfection
- Showing process > showing only results
- Genuine emotion > performed emotion
